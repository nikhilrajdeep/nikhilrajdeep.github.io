The person behind the pixels

I’m Nikhil, an applied scientist and developer connecting what we measure on the ground with what we observe from space.

I am a PhD candidate in Soil, Water, and Ecosystem Sciences at the University of Florida, working with Dr. Ebrahim Babaeian. My research brings together field observations, microwave remote sensing, soil physics and machine learning to understand water in the root zone—and the water available to crops.

Previously, as a Research Scientist at the National Remote Sensing Centre, ISRO, I led national-scale land-use and land-cover mapping with AWiFS and LISS-III and developed crop-mapping tools using Sentinel-1/2 and Google Earth Engine. My work also spans imaging spectroscopy for soil organic carbon and hyperspectral observations of mangrove chlorophyll.

The questions that guide my work

What is my research about?

  1. 01

    How much water lies beneath the surface?

    Retrieving field-scale root-zone soil moisture from satellite and UAV L-band radiometry, linked to field measurements and radiative-transfer physics.

  2. 02

    Can AI learn the physics of soil water?

    Building physics-guided models of soil-water retention and hydraulic conductivity, with uncertainty that makes predictions easier to interpret.

  3. 03

    How can observations support crop-water decisions?

    Connecting soil moisture, soil properties and crop conditions to forecast plant-available water in Florida’s sandy soils.

03 · Research

What I am working on

Microwave

Root-zone soil moisture from L-band radiometry

A calibrated τ–ω framework and an equilibrium-depth-scaling method that link surface brightness temperature to moisture deeper in the root zone, tested with a UAV L-band radiometer over potato fields at Citra, Florida.

SAR

NISAR soil-moisture validation

Evaluating NISAR L-band GCOV and the L3 soil-moisture products against about 80 in-field probe locations at four depths, using a water-cloud and integral-equation modelling approach.

Soil physics

Physics-informed pedotransfer functions

Neural networks that predict van Genuchten water-retention parameters and hydraulic conductivity from soil properties, with physical constraints and ensemble uncertainty — benchmarked against Rosetta on independent datasets.

Water & crops

Plant-available water forecasting

Forecasting plant-available water for Florida's sandy soils and cropping systems, connecting satellite observables to the agronomic water that crops can actually use.

Optical

Land cover & crop mapping

National LULC from AWiFS / LISS-III and seasonal crop-area mapping with Sentinel-1/2 on Google Earth Engine — the kind of mapping that turns satellite imagery like the globe above into classified maps.

Spectroscopy

Imaging spectroscopy of soil and mangroves

Soil organic carbon from PRISMA and AVIRIS-NG imagery, soil spectral libraries, and hyperspectral chlorophyll estimation in mangroves.
